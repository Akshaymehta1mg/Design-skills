# Understand the Design

Use this reference to evaluate whether the designer had enough understanding to make the decisions shown in the work.

## 1. Reconstruct the assignment

Summarise the supplied context without adding facts:

- **User:** who is affected
- **Situation:** what is happening when the need appears
- **Goal:** what the user is trying to accomplish
- **Current difficulty:** what prevents progress today
- **Product:** where the experience lives and what it can do
- **Outcome:** what should improve if the design works
- **Constraints:** business, policy, technical, time, platform, content, or system limits
- **Unknowns:** information that has not been supplied

If the brief contains conflicting statements, surface the conflict before evaluating the solution.

## 2. Review the designer’s questions

Do not count questions. Classify their usefulness:

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Useful question | Its answer materially informed the design direction |
| Already answered | The available context contained the information |
| Safe assumption | Proceeding was reasonable after stating the assumption |
| Important question missed | The answer could materially change the user story or solution |
| Unnecessary question | The answer would not affect a meaningful design decision |

Check whether the designer resolved enough uncertainty about:

### Problem

- What is happening today?
- Where does the difficulty occur?
- What is the user doing instead?
- What consequence makes the issue worth solving?
- Is the stated pain a symptom or the deeper design difficulty?

### User

- What situation brings the user into the experience?
- What do they already know?
- What are they trying to decide or accomplish?
- What creates hesitation, urgency, effort, or mistrust?
- Is the task new, repeated, occasional, or ongoing?
- What language, literacy, accessibility, environmental, or emotional factors may matter?

### Product

- Where does the experience begin and end?
- What information does the product already know?
- What can the product realistically explain, recommend, automate, or complete?
- Which existing patterns, channels, or systems must it connect with?
- What happens before and after the proposed experience?

### Constraints and risk

- Which business outcome matters?
- Which policy, legal, safety, privacy, or trust constraints affect the direction?
- Which technical limitations could change the flow?
- Which decisions are difficult or costly to reverse?

## 3. Judge whether a missed question matters

A missed question is important only when different plausible answers would lead to meaningfully different designs.

For each important missed question, state:

- The unresolved uncertainty
- The design decision that depends on it
- How the direction could change
- Whether evaluation can continue with a clearly stated assumption

Do not pause the review for non-critical unknowns. State the assumption and continue.

## 4. Reconstruct and review the user story

Write the story as a short sequence:

1. The user’s situation
2. The event or need that begins the journey
3. What the user tries to do
4. What prevents or complicates progress
5. What the user needs in order to move forward
6. What a successful outcome changes

Then assess:

- Which parts are directly supported by the context
- Which parts are reasonable inferences
- Which parts are unsupported
- Whether the proposed solution follows from this story
- Whether the story ignores a relevant user, stage, motivation, or consequence

Do not treat the user story as permanent. Invite correction when the supplied context is incomplete.

## 5. Output for this review layer

Return:

- A concise reconstruction of the assignment
- What the designer understood well
- Important questions handled well
- Important questions missed
- Assumptions that are safe to carry forward
- A corrected or clarified user story when needed
- The effect of any gap on the evaluation
