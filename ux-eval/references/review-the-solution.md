# Review the Solution

Use this reference to evaluate the strength of the proposed direction independently from the quality of its presentation.

## 1. Check the connection to the problem

Determine whether the proposal addresses:

- The user’s underlying difficulty rather than only a visible symptom
- The situation in which the difficulty occurs
- The user’s desired outcome
- The product outcome stated in the brief

Describe the solution’s central idea in one sentence. If that sentence cannot be written clearly, the direction may be fragmented or underdeveloped.

## 2. Check the fit with the user story

Ask:

- Would this user reasonably enter and use the experience in the proposed way?
- Does the direction account for what the user already knows?
- Does it reduce the user’s main uncertainty or effort?
- Does it respect the user’s emotional state, urgency, confidence, and environment?
- Does it demand behaviour, knowledge, attention, or trust the user may not have?

Mark any claim about real behaviour as an assumption unless supported by supplied research or observed evidence.

## 3. Identify the product’s role

Name the role the product is taking at each important stage:

- Informing
- Explaining
- Guiding
- Recommending
- Completing an action
- Acting within agreed limits
- Monitoring or supporting over time

Check whether the role is appropriate to the user’s need, product capability, risk, and expected level of control. Flag silent movement from one role to another.

## 4. Review the decision model

For each major user decision, assess:

- Whether the decision is necessary
- Whether it appears at the right time
- Whether the user has enough relevant information
- Whether alternatives and consequences are understandable
- Whether a default is appropriate and transparent
- Whether the user can reconsider or reverse the decision
- Whether the system could safely reduce effort without removing meaningful control

## 5. Review the complete journey

Examine:

- **Before:** how the need arises and why the user enters
- **During:** how the user understands, decides, acts, and recovers
- **After:** what changes, what remains visible, and what the next responsibility is

Decide whether the need is:

- A one-time task
- A repeated task
- An ongoing relationship

Flag a one-time flow that leaves an ongoing need unsupported, or an ongoing system that adds unnecessary weight to a simple task.

## 6. Review control, trust, and consequences

Check:

- What the product knows and how that is communicated
- What the product does automatically
- What requires permission or confirmation
- What can be edited, paused, cancelled, or undone
- What happens when information is incomplete or wrong
- Whether risks, commitments, and consequences appear before the action
- Whether the design creates pressure, obscures choice, or overstates certainty

Treat possible material harm, loss of control, privacy exposure, safety concerns, legal constraints, and irreversible consequences as potential blockers until appropriately addressed.

## 7. Review feasibility and connected systems

Identify assumptions about:

- Available data
- Product permissions
- Existing services and hand-offs
- Operational support
- Content ownership
- Technical behaviour
- Policy or regulatory approval

Do not reject a concept simply because implementation details are absent. Flag assumptions that could invalidate the direction.

## 8. Review exploration and trade-offs

Assess whether the designer:

- Considered meaningfully different directions when the problem allowed them
- Chose a direction for reasons connected to the user and product
- Made important trade-offs explicit
- Avoided adding features that do not strengthen the central idea

Do not demand multiple concepts when the task was narrow or the required pattern was already established.

## 9. Output for this review layer

Return:

- The central solution idea
- Strongest aspects of the direction
- Mismatches between the user story and the proposal
- Product-role concerns
- Important control, trust, feasibility, or consequence concerns
- Missing before, during, or after stages
- Trade-offs that need acknowledgment
- The most important revision to the direction
