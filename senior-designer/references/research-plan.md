# Research Plan

## What this step is for

You have a design direction but you're not sure it's right — or the team isn't sure. This is where you figure out what to test, how to test it, and what a good answer looks like. A research plan keeps the study focused on the actual product decision instead of turning into a general exploration.

## What the output looks like

```markdown
# Research Plan

## Research Objective
...

## Key Questions
- ...

## Hypotheses
- ...

## Participants
...

## Method
...

## Tasks or Stimuli
- ...

## What to Observe
- ...

## Success Signals
- ...

## Risks
- ...

## Timeline
...

## Output
...
```

## Picking the right method

- **Usability testing** when you need to know if people can use a screen or flow.
- **Interviews** when you need to understand needs, motivations, or mental models.
- **Concept testing** when you're exploring an early product direction and want gut reactions.
- **Surveys** only when the question is narrow and you already know the answer options.
- **Analytics review** when behavioral data already exists and can answer the question faster.

## Ground rules

- Tie the plan to the product decision it needs to inform. Research without a decision to feed is just curiosity.
- Don't over-research low-risk changes. If the stakes are small, the study should be small too.
- If the board is already specific, lean validation is usually enough — you don't need a full study for everything.
