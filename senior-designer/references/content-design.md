# Content Design

## What this step is for

The words in your interface are part of the design. Bad copy breaks a good layout. This step is for when you need to write (or rewrite) the actual text users see — headlines, button labels, error messages, empty states, onboarding text. The goal is copy that helps people understand what's happening and what to do next.

## How to think through it

1. **Where is the user right now?**
   - Learning something new
   - Making a decision
   - Taking an action
   - Waiting for something
   - Recovering from an error
   - Confirming something worked

2. **What job does this text need to do?**
   - Explain what's going on
   - Reassure them
   - Tell them what to do
   - Warn them about something
   - Confirm an action
   - Motivate them to keep going

3. **Write the copy:**
   - A clear headline that tells them where they are or what happened.
   - Useful body text — not filler, not marketing fluff.
   - A specific action label (not "Submit" — what are they actually doing?).
   - A helpful secondary action if they need an alternative path.

## Ground rules

- Use plain language. If your grandparent wouldn't understand it, rewrite it.
- Make CTAs action-specific. "Save changes" not "Submit." "Start free trial" not "Continue."
- Never blame the user in error messages. "We couldn't process that" not "You entered invalid data."
- Put the most important information first. Don't make people read three sentences to find out what happened.
- Match the user's confidence level. A first-time user needs more explanation than a power user.
