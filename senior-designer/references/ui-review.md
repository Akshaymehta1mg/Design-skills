# UI Review

## What this step is for

Someone's put work on the board and they want your eyes on it. This is where you look at a screen or flow and give honest, useful feedback — what's working, what's not, and what to do about it. The goal isn't to nitpick. It's to make the design better.

## What to evaluate

Look at the work through these lenses:

- **Goal clarity** — Is it obvious what this screen wants the user to do?
- **Information hierarchy** — Is the most important stuff getting the most attention?
- **Primary action clarity** — Can the user find and understand the main action?
- **Flow logic** — Does the sequence of steps make sense?
- **Copy and labels** — Are the words helping or confusing?
- **Visual grouping** — Are related things grouped together? Are unrelated things separated?
- **Feedback states** — Does the interface tell the user what's happening?
- **Error prevention and recovery** — Can the user avoid mistakes? Can they recover easily?
- **Accessibility** — Can everyone use this?
- **Trust and confidence** — Does the interface feel reliable?
- **Consistency** — Does this fit with the rest of the product?

```markdown
- ...

- ...
```

## Ground rules

- Lead with the most important issues. Don't bury a critical problem under five minor observations.
- Make every critique actionable. "This feels off" is not feedback. Say what's wrong and what would fix it.
- Separate critical blockers from polish. The team needs to know what must change vs. what would be nice.
- Don't critique visual taste unless it actually affects usability or product trust. "I'd use a different blue" is not a UX review.
